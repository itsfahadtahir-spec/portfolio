In buildFlagship · Full reconciliation platform, built with AI

Nodes

The problem: every month-end I matched thousands of lines by hand. The answer: I built the system.

At Canon and Pearl Initiative, reconciliations ate the first week of every month. ERP, payment provider and bank never agreed, and the fix was a person with a spreadsheet. I knew exactly what a good reconciliation looked like. I just couldn't code it.

With AI coding tools I could. Nodes is a working reconciliation platform I specified and built end to end: a deterministic matching engine that does the accounting, a control pack of 18 checks, a review queue where a reviewer signs off every exception, and an AI layer that explains each gap in plain English but never decides. Every decision leaves an audit trail.

  • Rules match, not the model
  • 18 controls in the pack
  • AI explains, a reviewer decides

Working Capital Agent

A 14 step n8n workflow, with K2 Horizon reasoning over the numbers.

I designed and wired the whole thing myself in a day: n8n reads the cash movements from four sources, forecasts the next 30 days with fixed rules, then hands the results to K2 Horizon to weigh up the fixes: chase a customer early, or ask a supplier to wait. A second AI step checks the first one's answer. The workflow pauses in Slack until a finance manager says yes, and only then drafts the email.

Skills on show: n8n workflow design, prompt and output validation for an LLM (K2 Horizon), Google Sheets and Slack and Gmail integrations, and a human approval gate designed by someone who has actually owned a cash forecast.

  • 14 steps in n8n
  • 30 days forecast ahead
  • 0 emails sent without a yes

02

How I got here, and what I bring.

I'm not the person who records the entries. I'm the person who turns the recorded numbers into a decision. Three teams taught me how; the rest, I built.

  1. 2016Canon · Dubai

    The 30 hour spreadsheet

    My first proper finance job. Every month I rebuilt a commission report by hand: USD 2.5m of sales across four tiers, five days of copying and checking. I moved it into Power BI. It now takes about three hours, and finance still signs off before anyone gets paid.

    Same place, I learned the unglamorous stuff properly: customer, vendor, bank and intercompany reconciliations, and an ERP migration where I tested the account mappings and moved the balances myself.

  2. 2021Pearl Initiative · Dubai

    Three audits, zero findings

    I swapped a multinational for a non profit and owned the books end to end in QuickBooks: monthly statements, annual statements, every schedule and reconciliation the auditors asked for. Three audits later, all closed on time, none with a finding.

    When a governance programme fell behind, I helped re plan it so six months of work landed inside the grant's final three months. That's where I learned that a good plan is also a finance tool.

  3. 2025LSN Signs · Dubai

    The week the cash runs low

    Now I'm at a growing SME, and the CEO plans around a rolling cash forecast I keep. The balance today is rarely the worry. The worry is the week a big supplier payment leaves before a customer pays. I spot those weeks and say who to chase and who to pay first.

    I also caught a 26% cost overrun mid project, traced it to supplier price rises and shipping delays, and changed how long a quote stays valid. Then I did something I hadn't done before: I started automating the parts of my job that were eating my week.

So I started building.

Not to leave finance. To put it at the front of the AI shift instead of waiting for it. Everything below started as a piece of finance work I used to do by hand, and now runs as an automation or an AI system I designed.
