01

Three finance teams. Same problem, different size.

Every team I've joined spent most of the month getting the numbers right and hardly any of it using them. Here's how that turned into what I do now.

  1. 2016Canon · Dubai

    The 30 hour spreadsheet

    My first proper finance job. Every month I rebuilt a commission report by hand: USD 2.5m of sales across four tiers, five days of copying and checking. I moved it into Power BI. It now takes about three hours, and finance still signs off before anyone gets paid.

    Same place, I learned the unglamorous stuff properly: customer, vendor, bank and intercompany reconciliations, and an ERP migration where I tested the account mappings and moved the balances myself.

  2. 2021Pearl Initiative · Dubai

    Three audits, zero findings

    I swapped a multinational for a non profit and owned the books end to end in QuickBooks: monthly statements, annual statements, every schedule and reconciliation the auditors asked for. Three audits later, all closed on time, none with a finding.

    When a governance programme fell behind, I helped re plan it so six months of work landed inside the grant's final three months. That's where I learned that a good plan is also a finance tool.

  3. 2025LSN Signs · Dubai

    The week the cash runs low

    Now I'm at a growing SME, and the CEO plans around a rolling cash forecast I keep. The balance today is rarely the worry. The worry is the week a big supplier payment leaves before a customer pays. I spot those weeks and say who to chase and who to pay first.

    I also caught a 26% cost overrun mid project, traced it to supplier price rises and shipping delays, and changed how long a quote stays valid. Then I did something I hadn't done before: I started automating the parts of my job that were eating my week.

So I started building.

Not to leave finance. To do it faster. Everything below started as a piece of finance work I used to do by hand.

What I've built

Three projects, each with its own room. Rules do the maths, AI explains, and a person always presses the button.

Hackathon buildn8n × K2 Horizon · Dubai · one day

Working Capital Agent

AED 85k in the bank on Friday. AED 55k by Wednesday.

This is my LSN problem, turned into a workflow. It reads the cash movements, forecasts the next 30 days with fixed rules, and asks K2 Horizon to weigh up the fixes: chase a customer early, or ask a supplier to wait. A second step checks the AI's answer. Then it pauses in Slack until a finance manager says yes, and only then drafts the email.

Nodes

Reconcile the volume. Review the exceptions.

Ten years of doing reconciliations by hand, and this is the tool I wish I'd had at every month-end. Nodes matches the bulk of transactions with deterministic rules, hands the exceptions to a reviewer with a plain English explanation, and keeps a full audit trail of who decided what.

Finds the next customer before sales has had coffee.

Signage companies live on new restaurants and cafés. This agent finds F&B operators in Dubai who may need signage, researches each one and drafts the first message. A person reads every draft before it goes out. Nobody gets a robot email with the wrong name on it.
